How much target information repairs a held-out phase?

The frozen cristobalite-beta LOPO readouts were scored on a common support that removes both few-shot calibration temperatures. The 1,675 K oracle-gap diagnostic and the predeclared 1,263 K one-label few-shot arm are distinct conditions; their boundary errors are 14.8/35.9 K and 26.6/70.7 K (C-simple/C-DPA), respectively. Two distant gap labels recover the broad trend, while an oracle slope sharply improves the boundary using eleven target temperatures.

Zero-shot

C-simple: 0.4398 meV/atom gap MAE. C-DPA: 1.1818 meV/atom. Both stable-envelope scores are 97.57%; both place the quartz/cristobalite boundary 39.8 K high.

Oracle gap · 1,675 K

The one-label oracle correction gives 0.4137/1.1815 meV/atom gap MAE (C-simple/C-DPA) and boundary errors of 14.8/35.9 K. The separate one-label few-shot arm consumes 1,263 K and gives 0.4961/1.4471 MAE with 26.6/70.7 K boundary errors.

Two target gaps

At 1,263 and 2,087 K, affine calibration reaches 0.0582 and 0.0862 meV/atom; boundary errors are 7.4 and 10.8 K.

A local oracle slope fixes the crossing for this held-out curve. Gap-plus-slope correction reduces MAE to 0.0790 meV/atom (C-simple) and 0.1159 meV/atom (C-DPA), with boundary errors of 0.87 K and 1.26 K, and no false cristobalite–tridymite crossing. The slope is fit from eleven target temperatures spanning 1,670–1,680 K, so this is a diagnostic information intervention, not a one-label prediction method.

The zero-shot scores reproduce the archived V2 full-support metrics; every scalar gap differs by at most 3.47×10⁻¹⁷ eV/atom. The oracle slope and few-shot rows are evaluated on the same fair support. The intervention applies a phase-level correction and derives all pairs from phase energies, preserving cycle closure.

Follow-up · transition identity and frozen curvature query

Re-scoring the recovered task-checkpoint models by ordered phase identity and direction changes the interpretation of the three-source result. On the fixed quartz–P6₃/mmc–cristobalite candidate pool, the two-source model correctly predicts quartz → cristobalite at 1,582.53 K (reference 1,542.71 K; 39.82 K error), with 97.57% stable-phase accuracy. The three-source model instead predicts quartz → tridymite at 1,582.52 K and tridymite → cristobalite at 1,823.66 K: zero correct identity matches, one missed reference event and two false events. Its identity-matched Tc error is not applicable. Target gap MAE falls 0.853 → 0.315 meV/atom, while the stable-phase decision does not improve.

The frozen curvature query adds six signed spectrum features without converting unstable modes into vibrational thermodynamics. For the two-source quartz–P6₃/mmc arm, gap MAE improves 0.853 → 0.793 meV/atom and slope error improves 1.521 → 1.394 µeV atom⁻¹ K⁻¹; stable-phase accuracy and the correctly identified transition are unchanged. For the three-source arm, gap MAE worsens 0.315 → 0.349 meV/atom and stable-phase accuracy falls 82.96% → 73.80%; its missed/false event counts remain 1/2.

Curvature evaluation used 4,892 force frames (212.5 s; 30.3 GB peak GPU allocation). Four-phase energy-force directional checks passed after accounting for single-precision energy resolution.
